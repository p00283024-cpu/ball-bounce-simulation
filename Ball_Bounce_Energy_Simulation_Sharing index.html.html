Student investigation
  1. Predict the velocity–time graph before starting. Where will the velocity change sign?
  2. Pause after the first impact. Compare velocities just before and just after contact.
  3. Set air drag to 0 and restitution to 1. What changes?
  4. Increase air resistance. How does the slope of the graph change on ascent versus descent?
  5. Explain why the ball reaches a lower height after each bounce using energy transfer.
Model note: Air resistance is modeled as quadratic drag, Fd = −½ρCdAv|v|, using a sphere of radius 0.035 m, Cd = 0.47, air density 1.225 kg/m³ and mass 0.20 kg. The drag slider scales this force. Impacts are modeled as instantaneous with vafter = −e vbefore; real impacts take a finite contact time. Consequently, the graph shows a sharp jump at each bounce.
